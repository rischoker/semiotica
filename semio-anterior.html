01 / THE SIGN

A word is not a thing.
It is a doorway.

Ferdinand de Saussure described the sign as two inseparable parts: the form we perceive and the concept it evokes. Meaning happens in the relationship between them.

02 / THE CONNECTION

Three ways a sign
speaks to us.

Charles Sanders Peirce classified signs by the relationship they have with what they represent. Look closely: resemblance, evidence, and convention are not the same thing.

Type 01Resemblance
✈

Icon

A sign that looks or sounds like its object. You can often recognise it without learning a special rule first.

EXAMPLE / An airplane pictogram
Type 02Evidence
〰

Index

A sign connected to its object by a real, often causal relationship. It points toward something happening or having happened.

EXAMPLE / Smoke suggesting fire
Type 03Convention
✳

Symbol

A sign whose meaning depends on a shared social rule or learned convention—not physical resemblance alone.

EXAMPLE / A dove representing peace
03 / READING IMAGES

Look again.
There is more beneath.

Roland Barthes explored how images communicate literal information and cultural values at the same time. An advertisement can show a product—and sell a whole idea of who you could become.

The reading method

What do you see?
What do you infer?

Tap the markers on the campaign visual. Separate what is literally present from the cultural message the design invites you to read.

DenotationThe literal, observable details: objects, words, colour, composition.
ConnotationThe associations and values shaped by culture, experience, and ideology.
